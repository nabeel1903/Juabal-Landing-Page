# Premium Landing-Page Design Prompt

Use this prompt for any modern SaaS, technology, AI, or service-business landing page. Replace the bracketed placeholders with project-specific information.

## Copy-Paste Prompt

> Design and build a premium, conversion-focused landing page for `[PRODUCT NAME]`, a `[SHORT PRODUCT DESCRIPTION]` created for `[TARGET AUDIENCE]`.
>
> The page’s primary conversion goal is `[PRIMARY GOAL]`. Use `[PRIMARY CTA]` as the dominant call to action and repeat it at appropriate points throughout the page. Use `[SECONDARY CTA]` as a lower-emphasis alternative. Keep For the messaging focused on the customer’s desired outcome rather than technical implementation details.
>
> Structure the page around AIDA:
>
> 1. Attention: communicate the core value immediately in the hero.
> 2. Interest: explain the product’s most important capabilities and differentiators.
> 3. Desire: demonstrate realistic use cases, workflows, outcomes, and trust signals.
> 4. Action: present the offer clearly and close with a strong final CTA.
>
> Create an original, high-end visual direction rather than a generic SaaS template. The experience should feel modern, composed, confident, and trustworthy. Use a light theme with warm off-whites, restrained neutral surfaces, deep readable text, and a controlled brand accent palette. Limited dark sections may be used to create contrast, but the overall experience should remain bright and approachable.
>
> Typography must feel editorial and distinctive. Use a high-quality modern display or sans-serif typeface, large confident headings, tight but readable tracking, clear hierarchy, and generous negative space. Keep headlines broad and concise; avoid narrow columns that create awkward five- or six-line headings. Body copy should remain comfortable to read and should never compete visually with the primary message.
>
> Build a memorable hero section with:
>
> - One concise eyebrow or category label
> - A bold outcome-oriented headline
> - A short supporting paragraph
> - One primary and one secondary CTA
> - A concise reassurance statement beneath the CTAs
> - A strong visual that demonstrates the product or customer experience
>
> Add a refined animated hero background. Suitable treatments include slow orbital lines, an abstract spatial grid, soft moving light, animated gradients, subtle particles, or a restrained code-built 3D field. Add gentle pointer parallax or layered depth to the main image and interface elements. Motion must feel cinematic and polished without reducing readability or distracting from the CTA.
>
> Use high-quality original images or illustrations that directly support the section where they appear. Generate custom assets when necessary. Maintain consistent lighting, color treatment, perspective, and art direction across the image set. Prefer credible editorial imagery over generic stock photography. Avoid decorative images with no content purpose, plastic-looking people, generic robots, hologram clichés, distorted anatomy, meaningless fake interfaces, fake text, and watermarks.
>
> Make the navbar modern and polished. Use a floating or softly elevated container, a clear brand lockup, compact navigation, and a visible primary CTA. Add subtle hover indicators, a smooth compressed state after scrolling, and an animated mobile menu. Ensure the navbar remains readable over every page section.
>
> Use purposeful interaction and motion throughout the page, especially in the hero, navbar, features, image cards, CTAs, and product demonstrations:
>
> - Stagger important entrances
> - Use restrained image parallax
> - Add polished button and link hover states
> - Introduce content based on scroll position
> - Include at least one advanced scroll sequence, such as pinned storytelling or sticky stacking cards
> - Use different motion patterns for different content types
> - Do not apply the same generic fade-up animation to every element
>
> Present key capabilities in a compact, gapless bento grid with intentionally varied column and row spans. Avoid a grid made entirely of identical cards. Combine concise copy with interface-style visuals, diagrams, timelines, calendars, waveforms, charts, before-and-after states, or outcome indicators created in code where appropriate. Keep the grid structured and information-rich without overcrowding it.
>
> When the product serves multiple audiences, industries, categories, or use cases, include an interactive horizontal image accordion:
>
> - Display four or five tall image panels in one row
> - Let the active panel occupy approximately 55–65% of the available width
> - Collapse inactive panels into narrow vertical image previews
> - Activate panels on hover, keyboard focus, or click
> - Animate expansion over approximately 600–700ms using `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`
> - Slightly zoom the active image
> - Add a subtle dark gradient for text readability
> - Reveal active-panel copy with a short delayed fade-and-slide animation
> - Display inactive labels vertically near the bottom
> - Place a small panel index in the upper-left corner
> - Convert the component into a vertical tap accordion on mobile
> - Support `aria-expanded`, visible keyboard focus, touch input, and reduced-motion preferences
>
> Demonstrate both the user experience and the resulting business outcome. For example, show an interaction on one side and the completed result, structured data, notification, booking, report, or workflow action on the other. Make the relationship between the product and the outcome immediately understandable.
>
> Explain the onboarding or operating process as a short sequence of clear steps. Use sticky or stacking-card storytelling when it improves comprehension, but simplify the interaction on mobile. Do not let animation obscure the order of the steps.
>
> If the product has pricing, present it clearly with plan differences, usage allowances, limitations, and additional charges. Visually emphasize one recommended plan without making the other choices difficult to understand. Never conceal important conditions in overly faint text.
>
> Add trust through truthful product evidence, realistic UI states, specific workflow examples, transparent pricing, security or compliance details when verified, and clear explanations of what happens after the user takes action. Do not invent testimonials, customer logos, performance statistics, certifications, integrations, or guarantees.
>
> Maintain excellent responsive behavior from wide desktop screens through small mobile devices. Preserve hierarchy, image focal points, CTA visibility, comfortable text sizing, and touch-friendly controls. Prevent horizontal overflow, clipping, layout shifts, unreadable overlays, and interaction states that work only with a mouse.
>
> Meet accessibility standards with semantic HTML, descriptive alt text, sufficient color contrast, visible focus states, keyboard-operable components, correctly associated form labels, meaningful button names, and `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.
>
> Apply independent design judgment throughout. These instructions establish the desired quality and behavior, but clarity, credibility, brand fit, readability, and conversion performance take priority over mechanically following a pattern.
>
> Implement the page using `[FRAMEWORK]` and `[ANIMATION APPROACH]`. Keep reusable components organized, centralize repeated content, optimize images, and avoid unnecessary dependencies. Start the application and verify the finished result at desktop and mobile sizes. Test navigation, CTA destinations, hover states, scrolling, interactive components, forms, keyboard behavior, production builds, accessibility basics, and browser-console errors.

## Suggested Placeholders

- `[PRODUCT NAME]`: The product or company name
- `[SHORT PRODUCT DESCRIPTION]`: A one-sentence explanation of the offer
- `[TARGET AUDIENCE]`: The primary customer group
- `[PRIMARY GOAL]`: Signup, free trial, demo request, purchase, or lead submission
- `[PRIMARY CTA]`: The main action label
- `[SECONDARY CTA]`: A lower-commitment alternative
- `[FRAMEWORK]`: Next.js, React, Webflow, Framer, or another platform
- `[ANIMATION APPROACH]`: GSAP/ScrollTrigger, Motion, CSS, or an equivalent system
